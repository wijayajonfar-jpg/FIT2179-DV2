Overview

Victoria’s public transport network

Victoria recorded 185 million public transport trips in 2025, but this number masks a dramatic story: a COVID‑19 collapse, followed by a strong but uneven recovery. This visualisation explores three connected questions: how patronage has changed over time, which transport modes carry the most passengers, and how usage compares across metropolitan and regional Victoria.
💡 Story goal: Understand how the pandemic reshaped travel behaviour and which parts of Victoria’s transport system have recovered fastest.
Chapter 1

The rise, fall and recovery of public transport

Public transport demand in Victoria changed dramatically between 2018 and 2026. The COVID‑19 pandemic caused an unprecedented drop, but recovery has been steady since 2022.

🔍 Key insight: Patronage fell by 72% from 2019 to 2020, but by 2025 had recovered to 97% of pre‑pandemic levels. The resilience of Victoria’s transport network is remarkable.

Metropolitan vs Regional Victoria

Most Victorians live in Melbourne, so it’s no surprise that metropolitan services dominate. But regional services are essential for connecting Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo and beyond.

Chapter 4

The pandemic’s impact (2020–2021)

COVID‑19 lockdowns and work‑from‑home orders caused the biggest disruption in Victorian public transport history. The drop was deepest in April 2020.

🦠 2019 vs 2020 vs 2021: a dramatic fall

🔍 Key insight: In April 2020, patronage fell by over 90% for some modes. The recovery began slowly in 2021 as restrictions eased.
Chapter 5

The road to recovery (2022–2026)

From 2022, as borders reopened and offices recalled workers, patronage climbed steadily. Some modes recovered faster than others.

Conclusion

What this shows overall

📌 Key findings

✅ Patronage collapsed during COVID‑19, falling 72% from 2019 to 2020, but recovered to 97% of pre‑pandemic levels by 2025.

✅ Metropolitan trains carry the most passengers (>40% share), while trams and buses each contribute ~20–25%.

✅ Metropolitan services dominate (~89% of trips), but regional V/Line trains move over 5 million people annually.

✅ Recovery has been uneven: trains bounced back fastest; buses and regional services have lagged, suggesting changed work and travel patterns.

✅ The network is Melbourne‑centric, but regional hubs are essential for statewide connectivity.
